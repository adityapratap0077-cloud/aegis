# Aegis design principles

These are the rules the design phase reasons from. They are constraints, not suggestions.

## Hierarchy

One clear hierarchy per page. The visitor should know what to look at first, second, and third within three seconds. If everything is emphasized, nothing is.

## Type

A real type scale with no more than four sizes. Body text at 16px or above on web, never below. One or two typefaces. Letterspacing is for labels, not paragraphs.

## Color

One primary, one accent, neutrals. Color carries meaning (state, hierarchy, brand), not decoration. Gradients only when they serve the brand. Dark mode must be designed, not inverted.

## Layout

Whitespace is a decision. Generous spacing beats dense packing. Align to a grid; ragged alignment needs a reason. Mobile layout is designed first-class, not a squeezed desktop.

## Template tells (banned by default)

Gradient headlines, glowing cards, tech-chip walls, floating orbs, and purple-blue dark mode soup. These are the marks of generated slop. If the user explicitly asks for one, it stops being slop and starts being a requirement. Otherwise, banned.

## Motion

Motion serves the content or it gets cut. Entrances guide the eye, transitions explain state changes, feedback confirms actions. No motion for its own sake. Respect prefers-reduced-motion.

## Content

Real copy, not lorem ipsum, before the design is called done. Headlines say what the thing is. Buttons say what happens next.
